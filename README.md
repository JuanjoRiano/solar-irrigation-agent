# Integrative Exam: Code, Data, and Results

Minimax + STRIPS + a Bayesian network. Sector: solar-powered irrigation scheduling.

## Running the Project

Requires Python 3.10 or later. From this directory:

```bash
python3 -m venv .venv
source .venv/bin/activate
python3 -m pip install -r requirements.txt
python3 agent.py
python3 test_agent.py
```

On Windows, activate with `.venv\Scripts\activate`. Execution uses the included copy of the data and does not require Internet access. To run only the calculations and log, without installing matplotlib: `python3 agent.py --no-plots`.

```bash
python3 agent.py --date 2025-01-24
python3 agent.py --date 2025-01-24 --prior 0.05 --output demo_prior_bajo
python3 agent.py --date 2025-01-24 --prior 0.60 --output demo_prior_alto
```

`--output` separates the outputs of each demo. The baseline posterior uses the fitted frequencies; `--prior` is a hypothetical experiment. Valid dates: 2025-01-01 through 2025-12-30. `--repeats 31` controls the number of benchmark repetitions; the median is reported in milliseconds. Timings depend on the machine and its workload.

## What Is Real and What Is a Design Choice

**Real data from a scientific source:** 2,192 daily NASA POWER records, point 4.2° N / 75.0° W, Tolima, 2020–2025, accessed on October 7, 2026. The original JSON, exact URL, and its SHA-256 are preserved. NASA POWER distributes estimates derived from satellite observations and reanalysis. These are not random values generated by this project, but they are not sensors installed on a farm either.

| Downloaded field | Unit according to the JSON | Use |
|---|---|---|
| ALLSKY_SFC_SW_DWN | kWh/m²/day | Daily horizontal solar radiation; not electricity generated by panels |
| PRECTOTCORR | mm/day | Corrected daily precipitation |
| T2M | °C | Record context; not used in the three-variable network |

**Calculated from these data:** median-based thresholds, counts, CPTs, posteriors, historical scenarios, and temporal validation.

**Academic design, not measurement:** network structure, the definition of “suitable,” smoothing, the inspection threshold, STRIPS operators, costs in points, relative coverage, and the Minimax adversary. The alternative priors in the sensitivity analysis are deliberate assumptions used to test stability. They are not attributed to NASA.

The success outcome is operationally defined as correctly identifying a weather window with low rainfall and high solar radiation on the following day. There are no records of flow rate, soil moisture, equipment failures, agricultural yield, or the physical success of irrigation. The posterior cannot be presented as the probability that a pump will work or that a crop will improve.

## Data Preparation and Evaluation Without Temporal Leakage

Missing values marked as `-999` or non-finite values are excluded; this download contains no missing values. Only pairs of consecutive days are formed. Medians are calculated using 2020–2024: solar radiation of 4.6807 kWh/m²/day and rainfall of 2.24 mm/day. The 75th percentile of rainfall (6.44 mm/day) is used only in the utility function.

Training: 1,826 pairs whose following day still falls within 2020–2024. Test: 364 pairs from 2025. The pair 2024-12-31 → 2025-01-01 is omitted to keep both sets separate, and the last day has no available future label. No outcomes from 2025 are used to fit the CPTs.

Benchmark scenarios are selected deterministically: the first test day for each $(C,E)$ profile, ordered by posterior; the minimum, middle, and maximum are retained. These are illustrative cases; predictive validation uses all 364 pairs, not just these three.

## Bayesian Network

- $C=1$: today's rainfall $\leq 2.24\;\mathrm{mm}/\text{day}$.
- $E=1$: today's solar radiation $\geq 4.6807\;\mathrm{kWh}/(\mathrm{m}^{2}\cdot\text{day})$.
- $R=1$: tomorrow meets both criteria simultaneously.

Network: $C\rightarrow E$, $C\rightarrow R$, and $E\rightarrow R$. The arrows describe a probabilistic factorization; they do not establish causality between rainfall and solar radiation.

$$
P(C,E,R)=P(C)\,P(E\mid C)\,P(R\mid C,E).
$$

Each binary CPT is estimated using Laplace smoothing, with $\alpha=1$:

$$
\widehat{P}=\frac{\text{favorable count}+1}{\text{context count}+2}.
$$

Smoothing prevents zero probabilities; it is a methodological choice, not an additional observation. `results/cpt_counts.csv` contains all counts and factors.

| $C$ | $E$ | $n(C,E)$ | $n(R=1,C,E)$ | $P(R=1\mid C,E)$ |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0 | 571 | 77 | 13.6126% |
| 0 | 1 | 340 | 65 | 19.2982% |
| 1 | 0 | 342 | 132 | 38.6628% |
| 1 | 1 | 573 | 299 | 52.1739% |

Joint probability example:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(C=1,E=0,R=1)
&=P(C=1)\,P(E=0\mid C=1)\,P(R=1\mid C=1,E=0)\\
&\approx 0.501094\times 0.374046\times 0.386628\\
&\approx 0.0724665\approx 7.2466\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

Demo case, 2025-01-24: rainfall 0.09; solar radiation 3.5616; $C=1$, $E=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(R=1)&\approx 31.4625\%,\\
P(R=1\mid C=1,E=0)&\approx 38.6628\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

The evidence raises the expectation by 7.20 percentage points; it does not guarantee the event. In fact, the actual label for the following day in this case is $R=0$.

In 2025: a Brier score of 0.17299 versus 0.19881 for the constant predictor based on the prior; lower is better. Accuracy at a 0.5 threshold: 73.35%. The “always $R=0$” baseline achieves 73.08%, because $R=1$ occurs on only 26.92% of test days. Therefore, accuracy is not used as the sole indicator of quality. There is no validation at other locations or in future years, nor any guarantee of calibration.

## STRIPS: Searching Rather Than Hardcoding a Route

Academic initial state:

$$
S_0=\{\text{base},\text{kit},\text{pendiente}\}.
$$

A different goal is constructed for each alternative: `solar_programado`, `respaldo_programado`, or `espera_registrada`. If $p<0.60$, where $p$ is the posterior, `sensores_revisados` is added. The 0.60 threshold is an illustrative policy; checking sensors does not automatically change the weather evidence.

The available operators have PRE, ADD, and DELETE sets in `OPS`. BFS explores states and finds a short plan; no predetermined sequence is stored as the solution. To avoid incompatible goals, each search allows only the terminal operator for its alternative. All three plans are validated by re-executing their preconditions and effects. “Inspect panels” is available, but does not help achieve these goals.

In the baseline case: go to the plot → verify equipment → check sensors → schedule solar irrigation. The graph shows the union of the three candidate plans; it does not aim to display every state explored by BFS.

## Minimax and Utility

MAX is the agent. MIN represents an adverse environment as a stress test, not a person or a probability distribution. Levels: MAX chooses solar/backup/postpone → MIN chooses an environment → MAX maintains/reinforces → MIN chooses the environment for the second window. The stress-test days are complete records near the 10th, 50th, and 90th percentiles of training solar radiation: 2022-11-20, 2021-03-29, and 2023-06-13. Rainfall and solar radiation from the same date are preserved. Combining windows creates counterfactual scenarios; they are not an observed historical sequence or a forecast.

The “reinforce” response is a contingent option in the future tree. STRIPS prepares the immediate decision. The principal variation of the baseline case chooses to maintain; no reinforcement is omitted from its immediate plan. The agent would need to observe again and replan before a future action.

For each window, $H$ is the solar radiation and $L$ is the rainfall in the historical record. $\operatorname{median}(H)$ and $Q_{0.75}(L)$ are calculated using the training data:

$$
\begin{aligned}
s&=\min\left(1,\frac{H}{\operatorname{median}(H)}\right),\\
d&=\max\left(0,1-\frac{L}{Q_{0.75}(L)}\right),\\
w&=0.5+0.5d.
\end{aligned}
$$

Relative coverage $q$ depends on the alternative $a$:

$$
q(a)=
\begin{cases}
s, & a=\text{solar},\\
\min(1,s+0.35), & a=\text{backup},\\
0, & a=\text{postpone}.
\end{cases}
$$

For active alternatives, reinforcement applies $q\leftarrow\min(1,q+0.20)$; when postponing, coverage remains $0$.

Utility uses the posterior $p$ and the average of the two evaluated windows:

$$
U=p\cdot\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}
\left[w_j\left(100q_j-40(1-q_j)\right)\right]
-\operatorname{cost}.
$$

Base costs in points:

$$
\operatorname{cost}_{\text{base}}(a)=
\begin{cases}
8, & a=\text{solar},\\
28, & a=\text{backup},\\
0, & a=\text{postpone}.
\end{cases}
$$

Reinforcement adds $12$ points to the base cost. **These are preference points**, not Colombian pesos or costs measured on a farm. Nor is $q$ photovoltaic efficiency, pumped water, or measured energy. The formula combines real environmental data with explicit preferences to compare alternatives; changing these weights can change the decision. The posterior is not a causal probability of an action and is used as a common contextual weight.

### Required Comparison

A total of $3\times3\times3=27$ combinations are evaluated: three scenarios, depths $\{2,3,4\}$, and three variants (naive, $\alpha$–$\beta$ pruning, and heuristic). The heuristic uses a horizon one level shorter ($d_{\text{effective}}=d_{\text{requested}}-1$, the `effective_depth` field) and fills in unobserved environments with the central record. Therefore, its savings include searching less; no equal-horizon comparison with the exact method is claimed. At depth 4, naive and alpha-beta reach full leaves and are directly comparable.

In all 27 cases, the decision is solar. Alpha-beta preserves the naive version's decision and value at the same depth. At depth 4, it reduces the number of expanded internal nodes from 31 to 17 (45.2% fewer) in these cases. Do not confuse expanded nodes with visited nodes: the complete tree has 67 nodes and 36 leaves.

The heuristic does not change the move in these three scenarios. Regret is calculated relative to the best full Minimax utility at depth 4, by re-evaluating the chosen action at that horizon. It is zero in these cases; this does not prove that it will always be zero. The CSV records every value and the actual timing on the execution machine.

## Sensitivity with Fixed Evidence

The prior $\pi=P(R=1)$ is varied across $0.05$, $0.20$, the fitted prior, $0.40$, $0.60$, and $0.80$. $P(C,E\mid R)$, calculated from the fitted joint distribution, is held constant.

With the same evidence, $C=1$, $E=0$, define:

$$
L_r=P(C=1,E=0\mid R=r),\qquad r\in\{0,1\}.
$$

The posterior for each alternative prior is calculated as:

$$
P_{\pi}(R=1\mid C=1,E=0)
=\frac{L_1\pi}{L_1\pi+L_0(1-\pi)}.
$$

Independence of $C$ and $E$ given $R$ is not assumed.

| Prior | Posterior | Decision | Steps |
|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 5% | 6.74% | Postpone | 3 |
| 20% | 25.56% | Solar | 4 |
| 31.46% (fitted) | 38.66% | Solar | 4 |
| 40% | 47.79% | Solar | 4 |
| 60% | 67.32% | Solar | 3 |
| 80% | 84.60% | Solar | 3 |

With a 5% prior, the goal is waiting + checked sensors. At 20%, solar irrigation is scheduled while retaining the inspection. At 60%, that inspection is no longer added. This shows that the decision is stable over part of the range and sensitive near the extremes and the policy threshold.

## Workflow and Deliverables

Observe the record → Bayes calculates the posterior → STRIPS prepares valid plans → Minimax decides → select the corresponding plan → record the recommendation. No physical equipment is activated.

- `agent.py`: commented implementation; `test_agent.py`: relevant checks.
- `data/`: original JSON, daily CSVs, pairs, URL, and `provenance.json`.
- `results/minmax_tree.png`: complete tree with values and the selected branch highlighted.
- `results/strips_graph.png`: states, plans, and legend.
- `results/bayes_bars.png`: posterior and evidence.
- `results/agent_summary.png`: a consistent summary of the plan, posterior, and decision.
- `results/agent_log.txt`: the same workflow printed to the console.
- `results/minimax_benchmark.csv`, `sensitivity.csv`, `cpt_counts.csv`, `test_predictions.csv`, `report.json`: a reproducible audit trail.
- `guion_demo.md`: explanation and five-minute demo.
